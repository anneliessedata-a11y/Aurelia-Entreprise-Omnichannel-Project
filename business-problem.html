BUSINESS PROBLEM

Customer retention is a decision problem before it is a modelling problem.

This is the business problem statement created for the flagship portfolio case study. It gives the analytical work a decision context rather than presenting the model as the starting point.

Problem statement. An omnichannel business has a large customer base but does not have a sufficiently structured view of which customer behaviours are associated with inactivity. The business needs evidence that can help it understand where retention attention may be warranted, which behavioural signals matter, and what information is still missing before a reliable intervention or forecasting system can be deployed.

The analytical objective is therefore not simply to “predict churn.” It is to connect customer, order, payment, returns, marketing and service behaviour into a coherent evidence base that can support retention investigation and prioritisation.

DECISION QUESTIONS

What should management be able to understand?

  • Which customer behaviours are associated with inactivity?
  • How does purchase frequency and recency relate to retention status?
  • What operational or service signals coexist with weaker customer outcomes?
  • Which customer segments warrant deeper investigation?
  • What additional data would be required for a genuinely forward-looking churn system?

ANALYTICAL BOUNDARY

What the current evidence cannot claim.

  • The supplied customer status field has no event timestamp.
  • The observed inactive rate is retrospective, not a deployment-ready forecast.
  • Association in the data should not be interpreted automatically as causation.
  • A model metric does not by itself establish commercial value or intervention effectiveness.
